# Interactive Prototype v0

This browser prototype is intentionally backend-free. Its job is to test whether the **relationship presence** concept is emotionally clear before production engineering begins.

## Run

Open `prototype/index.html` in a browser. No build step, package install, account, backend or API key is required.

## Primary walkthrough

1. Welcome — emotional promise and privacy boundary.
2. Create pair — invite one partner.
3. Pair accepted — equal-agency framing.
4. Shared home — local time, mood, status and next-meeting countdown.
5. Come Sit With Me — choose a realtime shared moment.
6. Incoming request — explicit accept/decline.
7. Together Now — simulated active session.
8. Session ended — clear non-recording state.
9. Our Story — lightweight shared timeline.
10. Your Controls — optional sharing, Private Time and disconnect.

## What to test

Do not explain the product before someone uses it.

Observe whether both partners independently understand:

- what the product is for,
- who controls each person's sharing,
- that a realtime request does not start automatically,
- that Private Time is normal rather than suspicious,
- why this feels different from messaging/video apps,
- and whether they would actually invite their partner.

## Interview prompts

After the walkthrough ask:

1. In one sentence, what do you think this app does?
2. Which screen made you feel closest to your partner?
3. Which screen made you uncomfortable?
4. What would you use tomorrow?
5. What would you remove completely?
6. Would you invite your partner tonight?
7. What app would this replace or reduce for you?
8. Would you pay £4.99, £6.99 or £8.99 per couple/month? Why?
9. What did `Private Time` mean to you?
10. What did you think happened when you pressed `Come sit with me`?

## Prototype pass criteria

Proceed to the thin technical vertical slice only if research broadly supports all of the following:

- the purpose is understood without founder explanation,
- pair invitation feels natural,
- both people can explain the consent model,
- the shared-home concept creates a distinct emotional response,
- the strongest demand is closeness rather than monitoring,
- and enough couples take a real next action to justify engineering.

## Deliberate exclusions

This prototype does not implement:

- background device monitoring,
- hidden camera or microphone use,
- message interception,
- social-media inbox access,
- cheating detection,
- partner scoring,
- public profiles,
- payments,
- production authentication,
- production location,
- production media capture.

These exclusions are product boundaries, not missing prototype polish.
