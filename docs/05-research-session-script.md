# 20-Couple Research Session Script

## Rule zero

Do not sell the idea during the session.

The moderator is testing whether the product communicates its own value. If the participant needs repeated founder explanation, record that as product failure rather than correcting them into the desired answer.

## Session format

Target: 25–35 minutes per couple.

### Part 1 — Before prototype
Ask each partner separately where possible:

1. How long have you been long distance?
2. What is the hardest ordinary part of being apart?
3. What apps do you use today to stay close?
4. What question do you repeatedly ask each other because you are not physically together?
5. Do you currently share location, calendar, photos, widgets or other persistent context?
6. What causes unnecessary misunderstandings because of distance?

Do not mention monitoring, transparency or tracking unless the participant introduces those ideas.

### Part 2 — Silent prototype walkthrough
Give the participant the prototype and say only:

> “Imagine your partner sent you this or you discovered it yourself. Use it as you naturally would.”

Do not guide them through the navigation.

Observe:
- first interpretation,
- hesitation points,
- whether they invite/connect naturally,
- reaction to Right Now,
- reaction to Come Sit With Me,
- reaction to explicit accept/decline,
- reaction to Our Story,
- reaction to Private Time,
- reaction to Disconnect.

### Part 3 — Comprehension
Ask:

1. In one sentence, what does this app do?
2. Who controls Maya's sharing?
3. What happens when Alex sends “Come sit with me”?
4. What does Private Time do?
5. If one person disconnects, what do you expect to happen?
6. Did anything feel like surveillance or checking up on someone?

### Part 4 — Behavioural value
Ask:

1. Which screen would you genuinely use tomorrow?
2. Which screen would you probably ignore?
3. Which feature would reduce your use of another app?
4. What ordinary moment do you wish you could share more easily today?
5. Would you invite your partner tonight if this were real?
6. Would your partner want this as much as you do?
7. What would make either of you uninstall it?

### Part 5 — Feature pressure test
For each concept ask **use / maybe / never**:

- Right Now mood/availability,
- local time/time difference,
- meeting countdown,
- tiny photo/moment,
- voice “Come sit with me”,
- video,
- user-started screen presentation,
- optional location,
- Our Story,
- shared plans,
- Private Time.

Do not interpret “maybe” as demand.

### Part 6 — Pricing
Test one price per couple first to avoid anchoring the entire cohort to a menu.

Rotate across interviews:
- £4.99/month per couple,
- £6.99/month per couple,
- £8.99/month per couple.

Ask:

> “Imagine the app worked reliably and the features you said you would use were included. Would the two of you actually pay this monthly?”

Then:
- Why?
- Who would pay?
- What would have to be included?
- What would make the price feel ridiculous?

After the answer, the full price range may be discussed.

## Strong positive evidence

- both partners independently understand the product,
- both can explain independent control,
- invitation feels natural,
- one or more features solve an existing repeated behaviour,
- they ask how to get the real beta,
- they perform a real waitlist/invite action,
- willingness to pay survives a concrete price.

## Weak evidence

- “cool idea”,
- “I would probably use it”,
- compliments to the founder,
- one partner loves it while the other is visibly uncomfortable,
- hypothetical willingness to pay without action,
- interest only in monitoring/location.

## Research stop conditions

Pause and redesign before completing all 20 if the first 6–8 couples consistently:
- misunderstand the app as surveillance,
- cannot explain who controls sharing,
- see no value beyond WhatsApp + existing tools,
- feel partner invitation would cause suspicion,
- or show strong one-sided adoption.

## Decision output

At the end of 20 couples produce exactly one recommendation:

**BUILD** — emotional job and pair adoption are clearly demonstrated.

**RESHAPE** — problem is real but product/positioning is wrong.

**KILL** — existing tools are sufficient or demand is primarily unhealthy monitoring.
