# Product Thesis

## The problem

Long-distance relationships lose ambient context. Couples living together naturally receive small signals all day: whether the other person is home, busy, awake, relaxed, commuting, cooking, or simply available. Distance strips many of those signals away and replaces them with information gaps.

All I Need Is Love is designed to restore some of that everyday sense of presence without asking either partner to give up control of their own information.

## The category

All I Need Is Love is a **relationship presence platform** for two consenting adults who want to feel closer while living apart.

It combines five layers:

1. **Ambient Presence** — local time, mood, availability, distance, optional location, arrival moments, battery and next-meeting countdowns.
2. **Shared Moments** — user-initiated voice, video, screen sharing, watch-together and lightweight co-presence.
3. **Shared Life** — photos, notes, voice clips, rituals, plans, dates, wish lists, future trips and memories.
4. **Consent Engine** — clear sharing choices with scope, duration, history and easy revocation.
5. **Intelligence** — later-stage translation, memory search, planning and relationship-aware assistance.

## Core promise

> **Presence, not possession.**
>
> Two people can voluntarily open controlled windows into their lives while each person remains in charge of what they share.

## Product doctrine

- Consent is a product primitive, not an afterthought.
- Both partners have equal agency.
- The product remains useful when optional sharing features are disabled.
- Prefer temporary, purpose-specific sharing when the use case allows it.
- Declining a request must never produce punishment or pressure.
- Relationship disconnect immediately removes relationship-scoped access.
- Realtime media is not recorded by default.
- Privacy controls must use plain language.
- Romantic design must never blur a permission boundary.

## User jobs

The user is hiring the app to:
- reduce the emotional distance between us,
- make ordinary life feel shared,
- reduce repetitive check-in questions,
- create little rituals and memories,
- make time-zone separation easier,
- and connect future in-person meetings to an ongoing shared life.

## North-star metric

**Weekly Mutual Presence Sessions per paired couple.**

A session counts when both partners actively participate in a shared interaction or mutually enabled presence feature. Supporting metrics:

- invite-to-pair conversion,
- D7 and D30 paired-couple retention,
- couples with 3+ mutual sessions per week,
- successful expiry/revocation of temporary grants,
- disconnect/report rate,
- invite acceptance time,
- paid conversion per couple.

## MVP hypothesis

> Long-distance couples will repeatedly use a small set of consent-first ambient and realtime features because those features create a stronger feeling of shared life than stitching together multiple general-purpose apps.

## Kill criteria

Do not build the large platform if user research repeatedly returns:
- “My existing apps already do enough.”
- “I would not invite my partner.”
- “The shared-presence features do not make us feel closer.”
- “I would not pay even a small couple subscription for this.”

The desired language from users is **closer, together, present, connected, calm, shared**.
